# Evangelizing and Scaling Research

## Executive Summary 
Across my career, I have joined teams either immediately prior to after a significant org change. At **Groundswell** I worked with UX leadership to craft intentional UX Research applications as part of our responses to federal RFPs for work. At **Cisco** I had the opportunity to build a dedicated research team after a significant reorg. At **Bright MLS** I shifted and scaled the team from "pushing pixels" to clarifying intent. And at **Capital One** I supported scaling the research team within the Developer Experience pillar while connecting knowledge around data and artificial intelligence teams as well as scaling understanding across multiple platform teams. 

## Approach (The What) 
In each case, I started with the team, leveraging documents such as a *Today and Beyond* document to understand how my team and peers like to work and scaling to a *Team Vision / Charter*. 

I completed this in conjunction with a series of deep dive 1:1s across my teams and the broader organizations to understand wants, needs, and motivations. (As an aside, people often ask how I feel "leaving" the research as I have moved into leadership. I still research, my scope has shifted to understanding the organization as much as the product.)

## The Outcomes (The So What)
While different at each organization, some common threads have included: 
- Organizational or team maturity maps 
- Scalable and repeatable trainigns 
- Clear, self-service documentation 

## The Impact (The Now What)
Where is is sometimes difficult to quantify direct monetary gain from a research decision when there are a number of business decisions between Insight and Action, my most common measure of value is name recognition and repeat customers. 

**Name Recognition** Do people know the researchers and designers by name? Are they sought out? Across my career, the team impact and visibility increases. 

**Repeat Customers** Related to this, the more people recognize our name, the more people come back for more research and engagement. This is our greatest win both at an individual, emotional front, and proving the value of research to speed and and streamline product development. 

## Lesons Learned 
One of the consistent challenges was around organizational hierarchy. In this, it is sometimes difficult to get in the room with key partners if your title leaves a power dynamic to your intended audience. 

As I progressed through my roles, I continued to partner my direct leadership, and colleagues in other disciplines, empowering them to champion the work where I might not have been present. 

This setting up others to succeed, or "rising tide raises all ships" cliche is true for evangelizing and scaling research.
